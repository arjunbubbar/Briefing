Rates & Inflation Pulse

Global sovereign yields surged this week as the Iran-triggered energy shock revived inflation fears. The US 30-year Treasury rose to 5.114% — its highest since May 2025 — while the UK 10-year gilt closed at 5.18%, up 14 basis points on the day, as traders priced a premium for BoE caution and persistent domestic inflation above the 2% target. Markets now assign less than 10% probability to a Fed cut at the June FOMC meeting, down from roughly 40% at the start of the week; the Fed funds target range of 3.50–3.75% is expected to hold through the summer under new Chair Kevin Warsh, who replaced Jay Powell this month. The BoE's 4.25% Bank Rate is similarly seen on hold through Q3 2026: with Brent crude at $109 per barrel, energy-driven second-round effects threaten to push UK CPI — already at 3.3% — above 3.5% by autumn, complicating any near-term easing narrative.

Glossary

Bear steepening
When longer-maturity bond yields rise faster than shorter-maturity yields — both moving higher, but the long end surging more. This week's move (US 30Y to 5.11%, UK 10Y to 5.18%) is a textbook bear steepener: it signals markets are pricing in persistently higher inflation rather than growth optimism, and it typically raises borrowing costs for mortgages, corporate debt, and governments alike.
Wafer-scale integration (WSI)
An AI chip architecture where an entire semiconductor wafer is used as one giant processor rather than being cut into hundreds of individual dies. Cerebras Systems pioneered this approach: its CS-3 chip contains 4 trillion transistors on a single 300mm wafer, enabling much faster data movement between compute units than a multi-chip GPU setup — a significant advantage for AI inference workloads.
Backwardation
When the spot (front-month) price of a commodity trades above the price for future delivery. Oil is in deep backwardation this week — Brent spot at $109 versus 6-month forward prices near $98 — signalling that markets see severe near-term supply tightness (from the Hormuz closure) but expect some easing over the medium term. Backwardation rewards holders of physical oil and roll-yield strategies.
