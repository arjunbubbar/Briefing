Why now: Brent sustaining above $100 — supported by a structural OPEC+ output discipline and now an acute geopolitical disruption — provides the highest-confidence earnings backdrop for XOM in three years. The stock's 14× forward multiple is at a modest discount to the S&P 500, offering energy-sector exposure with a defensible dividend and buyback floor.

Risks: (1) A durable US-Iran ceasefire would push Brent back toward $80, compressing earnings forecasts by 20–25%. (2) Accelerated US shale production from other operators could create supply overhang in H2 2026, pressuring medium-term crude prices.

Bear in one line: XOM's premium valuation relative to energy peers embeds a geopolitical risk premium that deflates quickly if diplomacy succeeds.

9. Glossary

Strait of Hormuz
A narrow waterway between Iran and Oman through which roughly 21 million barrels of oil per day flow — about 20% of global seaborne crude supply. Any closure or military threat in the strait drives crude prices sharply higher and widens energy risk premiums across global markets.
Nonfarm Payrolls (NFP)
The US Bureau of Labor Statistics' flagship monthly employment report, counting net jobs added or lost across the economy (excluding agriculture, private households and some nonprofit workers). Released on the first Friday of each month, it is one of the most-watched data points for Federal Reserve policy decisions.
TTF (Title Transfer Facility)
The benchmark virtual trading hub for natural gas in the Netherlands and the primary reference price for European gas contracts and LNG spot imports. Quoted in €/MWh; a rising TTF reflects either supply fears, low storage, or cold-weather demand — all of which apply in the current geopolitical environment.
