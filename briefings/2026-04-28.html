US Pick: ExxonMobil (XOM) — BULL CASE

The world's largest publicly traded energy company is a direct beneficiary of the Strait of Hormuz crisis, with integrated operations that turn $110+ Brent into outsized free cash flow.

Stat Value
Price (28 Apr 2026 close)~$136.50
Market Cap~$580bn
Fwd P/E (2026E)~12.1×

Bull Points:

  1. Strait premium locks in super-normal cash flow. With Brent above $110/bbl and ExxonMobil's upstream break-even around $35–40/bbl, each $10 move in oil adds roughly $3–4bn in annual free cash flow. The Hormuz blockage — disrupting ~20% of global seaborne oil trade (Vanguard, Apr 2026) — could sustain elevated prices for 12–18 months.
  2. Integrated refining absorbs volatility. Unlike pure-play E&Ps, ExxonMobil's downstream refining and chemicals divisions can convert cheap natural gas feedstocks into higher-margin products. This natural hedge dampens downside and amplifies crack-spread gains when crude is high.
  3. Pioneer acquisition synergies accelerating. The 2024 Pioneer Natural Resources acquisition gave ExxonMobil low-cost Permian Basin inventory; management guided for $3bn+ in synergies by 2026 and the assets continue to generate strong returns even at sub-$70 oil — making current prices look extraordinarily profitable.
  4. Shareholder returns engine. ExxonMobil raised its dividend for the 42nd consecutive year and operates a $20bn+ share buyback programme. With free cash flow yield approaching 10% at current oil prices, capital return to shareholders provides a floor on valuation (Bloomberg, Apr 2026).

Why Now: Brent at $111/bbl on the back of geopolitical supply disruption — precisely the macro environment that maximises ExxonMobil's earnings power. The FOMC hold preserves the dollar's yield attraction without strengthening it enough to crush commodity prices. Q1 2026 earnings (due early May) are set to be blockbuster, providing a near-term catalyst.

Risks: (1) A sudden US–Iran diplomatic breakthrough could cut $20–30/bbl off Brent overnight, compressing margins sharply. (2) An accelerated global energy transition or a US recession could deflate oil demand beyond current consensus forecasts.

Bear in one line: A diplomatic resolution to the Hormuz crisis and a US hard landing could see XOM re-rate toward 9–10× earnings within six months.

Shell trades at roughly 9.8× 2026 forward earnings versus ~12× for US majors despite comparable integrated-operations quality, a higher dividend yield, and similar buyback yield.   3. $3.5bn quarterly buyback maintained. Shell has committed to returning at least $3.5bn per quarter to shareholders via buybacks at $70+ Brent. At $111/bbl, incremental free cash flow will likely push buybacks above guidance, providing mechanical earnings-per-share accretion through 2026.
  4. Integrated chemicals and lubricants buffer. Shell's Chemicals & Products division — producing petrochemicals, lubricants and refinery products — benefits from wide oil-to-product spreads in a high-crude environment, diversifying earnings away from pure upstream exposure and supporting the dividend through the cycle (Reuters, Apr 2026).

Why Now: Brent at $111, UK gilt yields at 4.95% (squeezing growth stocks), a FTSE 100 underperforming on macro concerns — all three dynamics push value-oriented, high-yield, commodity-linked names like Shell higher relative to the index. Q1 2026 results are expected in early May and should confirm blockbuster upstream earnings.

Risks: (1) European activist pressure and net-zero litigation risk remain overhangs on the re-rating potential. (2) A rapid Hormuz resolution or coordinated IEA strategic petroleum reserve release could knock $20+/bbl off Brent, cutting free cash flow and buyback capacity.

Bear in one line: A geopolitical de-escalation plus IEA reserve release could see Shell's 2026 earnings estimates cut 25–30%, eliminating the buyback yield advantage and triggering a re-rate lower.

9. GLOSSARY

Term Plain English
Strait of Hormuz The narrow waterway between Iran and Oman through which roughly 20% of global seaborne oil trade passes. When it is disrupted, oil prices spike immediately because there is no quick alternative route for Persian Gulf exports.
Term Premium The extra yield investors demand for holding a long-term bond (e.g. a 10-year gilt) instead of rolling over a series of short-term loans. When governments borrow a lot and inflation is uncertain, the term premium rises — pushing long-term yields higher even without central bank rate hikes.
Advance GDP Estimate The first official reading of a quarter's economic growth, published roughly 30 days after the quarter ends. It is based on incomplete data and is typically revised twice (to a "second" and then "final" estimate) as more complete figures arrive. Markets react strongly to the advance estimate even though it often changes.
