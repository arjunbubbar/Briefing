RATES & INFLATION PULSE

UK gilt markets are repricing toward crisis levels, with the 10-year yield touching 5.00%—levels last seen in 2008—as the oil-driven CPI surge to 3.3% in March far exceeded the 3.1% consensus and all but extinguished hopes of a Bank of England rate cut this year; Lloyds now forecasts UK inflation at 3.4% for 2026 with GDP growth of just 0.5%, a stagflationary combination that leaves the MPC trapped between growth and price stability. Across the Atlantic, the Federal Reserve held the fed funds rate at 3.50–3.75% in its most divided vote since 1992, with Powell reaffirming his commitment to remain on the Board of Governors; the US 2-year Treasury jumped to 3.93% from 3.84%, and fed funds futures have stripped out nearly all 2026 easing bets, now pricing a small chance of a hike instead. The common thread is the oil shock: with Brent above $118 and TTF gas up over 10% on the session, energy-driven inflation is entrenching across both economies, leaving central banks with no good options and bond markets repricing duration risk sharply.

  • BlackRock Investment Institute, Weekly Commentary (30 Apr 2026): "Long-duration government bonds are failing in their traditional role as portfolio diversifiers as the term premium rises with sustained inflation and elevated debt loads — the 10-year gilt yield hitting 5% is a symptom of this structural shift, not an anomaly. GLOSSARY

Term Premium
The additional yield investors demand to hold a long-dated bond (e.g. a 10-year gilt) rather than repeatedly rolling short-term bills. It compensates for uncertainty about future inflation and interest rates; it rises when long-term risks increase — as with the UK 10-year gilt yield hitting 5.00% today on oil-driven inflation fears.
Strait of Hormuz Blockade
Military or naval control of the Strait of Hormuz, a narrow waterway between Iran and Oman through which roughly 20% of globally traded oil and 25% of LNG normally flows. US naval enforcement of Iranian export restrictions has sharply reduced throughput, creating the supply shock behind Brent's rise above $118/barrel.
PCE Deflator
The Personal Consumption Expenditures price index — the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation measure. Unlike CPI, it accounts for consumers substituting cheaper goods when prices rise, producing a generally lower reading. The Fed's 2% inflation target is set in PCE terms, making it the key number FOMC members watch when deciding whether to hold, cut, or hike rates.
