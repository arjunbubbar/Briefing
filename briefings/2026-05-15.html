🇺🇸 US Pick — Nvidia (NVDA)  Bull Case

The undisputed picks-and-shovels play on the global AI infrastructure buildout, Nvidia sits at the centre of today's biggest market catalyst: CEO Jensen Huang has joined President Trump's Beijing delegation, raising the prospect of a deal to ease AI chip export restrictions to China — a market that was generating ~$15 bn in annual revenue before controls tightened.

  1. China market re-opening catalyst. Jensen Huang's presence in the Trump–Xi delegation signals that AI chip access is on the official agenda. Any relaxation of H20/Blackwell export controls would unlock an addressable market analysts estimate at $20–25 bn per year — roughly 15% of FY2026 data-centre revenue — with virtually no incremental cost (Bloomberg, 15 May 2026).
  2. Insatiable data-centre demand. Hyperscalers — Microsoft, Google, Amazon and Meta — have collectively guided to $400 bn+ in combined 2026 capital expenditure, with Nvidia estimated to capture 60–70% of AI accelerator spend. Blackwell Ultra (B300) shipments began in Q1 2026 and order backlogs stretch well into 2027 (Reuters, April 2026).
  3. Agentic AI multiplies compute demand. The industry shift from large-language-model inference to multi-agent, continuous-reasoning systems requires orders-of-magnitude more GPU hours. Nvidia's CUDA software moat deepens with each new generation, making switching costs prohibitively high for hyperscalers already embedded in the ecosystem (Bloomberg Intelligence).
  4. Sovereign AI tailwind. Governments in Europe, the Middle East and South-East Asia are building national AI infrastructure. Nvidia has signed sovereign AI deals worth >$10 bn since 2025, providing a diversified revenue stream less sensitive to US–China trade tensions (Bloomberg, Q1 2026 earnings call).

Why now: The Beijing summit is a binary, near-term catalyst — any positive signal on chip-access could drive a sharp re-rating. Shares have already rallied 1.8% in today's session on summit optimism, but the market is pricing a cautious outcome; a concrete deal announcement would be incremental upside. Technically, NVDA has broken above its April consolidation range.

Risks: (1) Talks break down or export restrictions tighten — removing the China optionality premium embedded in current valuation. (2) Custom silicon from Google (TPU), AWS (Trainium) and in-house hyperscaler chips gain training share, pressuring Nvidia's pricing power over a 2–3 year horizon.

Bear in one line: At 34.8× forward earnings, any demand or margin miss — or a failed Beijing summit — compresses the multiple sharply, given the stock already embeds near-perfection.

Glossary

Strait of Hormuz
A narrow waterway between Iran and Oman through which roughly 20% of global seaborne oil and a significant share of LNG transits each day. Its effective closure — as during the current US–Iran conflict — creates an immediate supply shock, pushing Brent crude sharply higher and forcing tankers onto longer, more expensive alternative routes.
PPI (Producer Price Index)
A measure of inflation at the wholesale level — the prices businesses charge each other before goods reach consumers. Because PPI changes tend to feed through into CPI with a lag of 1–3 months, a sharp PPI overshoot (as seen today: +1.4% vs +0.4% expected) is treated by bond markets as a leading indicator of stickier consumer-price inflation.
Forward P/E
A valuation ratio calculated by dividing a company's current share price by its expected earnings per share over the next 12 months. Unlike trailing P/E (which uses historical earnings), forward P/E reflects market expectations of future profitability; a high forward P/E signals that investors are paying a premium for anticipated growth.
