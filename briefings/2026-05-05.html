US: eBay Inc. (EBAY) — Bull Case

GameStop's unsolicited $56bn takeover offer has placed a hard floor under eBay's valuation — here is why the bull case holds even if the deal fails.

Metric Value
Price (5 May 2026) ~$109
Market Cap ~$46.2bn
Fwd P/E ~18x
Dividend Yield ~1.8%

Bull Points:

  1. Hard valuation floor at $125. The $125/share offer — a ~20% premium to Friday's close of ~$104 — establishes a reference price that competitors or private equity sponsors could match. Even without GameStop closing the deal, precedent bids have historically repriced C2C e-commerce platforms at a premium. (Source: Bloomberg, 4 May 2026)
  2. Category leadership in collectibles and gaming. eBay generates more than 30% of gross merchandise value from collectibles, trading cards, and gaming hardware — the exact verticals where GameStop's 1,600 physical US stores have built deep customer trust. A merger would be genuinely additive in these segments, and even standalone, eBay's dominance here is underappreciated by the market. (Source: CNBC, 5 May 2026)
  3. $2bn annual synergy commitment on the table. Ryan Cohen's cost-cutting playbook at GameStop reduced the company's overhead by nearly 40% without sacrificing revenue; applying the same discipline to eBay's notoriously bloated SG&A could drive substantial margin expansion — independent of deal completion. (Source: Bloomberg / Fortune, 4–5 May 2026)
  4. Compelling spread opportunity. eBay trades at ~$109 versus the $125 offer price — a ~15% spread. With a $20bn TD Bank financing commitment publicly disclosed and GameStop signalling it can issue equity to bridge the gap, the deal is not purely theoretical. A spread narrowing to $120+ represents meaningful upside over any 6–12 month horizon. (Source: CNBC, 4 May 2026)

Why Now: The unsolicited offer has reset the market's perception of eBay's strategic value. The stock has traded in a tight range for 18 months as investors treated it as a mature, slow-growth asset. A $125 reference price — now public — changes the negotiating landscape, and any counterbid from a strategic or financial buyer would add further upside.

Key Risks:

  • Financing gap: GameStop's own market cap is only ~$12bn against a $56bn bid; despite the TD Bank letter, significant equity dilution or deal collapse remains a real risk. Michael Burry, a GameStop holder, called the strategy "pedestrian." (CNBC, 5 May 2026)
  • Antitrust scrutiny: A horizontal deal of $56bn in the e-commerce space would face intense DOJ review, and the regulatory timeline could extend well into 2027, introducing material uncertainty for arb investors.

Bear Case in One Line: If GameStop's bid collapses — the base case for most traders — eBay likely retreats to ~$90–95, representing ~13–17% downside from current levels.

UK: BP plc (BP.L) — Bull Case

With Brent above $113/bbl and the Strait of Hormuz partially disrupted, BP's integrated cash-flow engine looks compelling on a relative basis.

Metric Value
Price (5 May 2026, est.) ~560p
Market Cap ~£87bn
Fwd P/E ~8.5x
Dividend Yield ~5.5%

Bull Points:

  1. Exceptional free cash flow at elevated oil prices. BP generates roughly $2bn additional quarterly FCF for every $10/bbl sustained increase in Brent. With oil at $113.85 versus BP's internal planning assumption of ~$70, the company is generating transformational excess cash that funds buybacks and reduces net debt simultaneously. (Reuters, May 2026)
  2. Structural discount to US peers. BP trades at ~8.5x forward earnings versus Exxon's ~12x — a 30% valuation gap that has persisted despite comparable return-on-capital metrics. The discount partly reflects UK-market sentiment and ESG-transition concerns, but at current oil prices neither is an immediate near-term risk. (Bloomberg Markets)
  3. Committed buyback programme. BP's ongoing share repurchase programme reduces the share count and increases EPS mechanically, providing a partial hedge against any future oil-price softening. The 5.5% dividend yield is 45bps above the UK 10Y gilt, an unusual premium versus gilts that historically attracts income-seeking institutional flows. (BP investor relations)
  4. Geopolitical risk premium embedded in Brent. The Strait of Hormuz supplies roughly 20% of global oil exports; even a partial resolution of the conflict will take time, keeping supply risk premia elevated for quarters, not weeks. BP's upstream portfolio has limited direct Hormuz exposure, meaning it captures the price benefit without the operational risk. (CNBC / Reuters, 5 May 2026)

Why Now: The oil price shock is not ephemeral; the Strait of Hormuz blockade scenario implies a sustained supply disruption that BP's balance sheet and integrated model are perfectly positioned to monetise, at a valuation multiple that still doesn't price in a prolonged elevated price environment.

Key Risks:

  • Energy transition regulatory risk: UK and EU mandates on fossil-fuel phase-out create long-term headwinds for oil majors' terminal value and capital allocation credibility.
  • Rapid oil-price mean reversion: A diplomatic breakthrough in the Strait of Hormuz — however unlikely today — could see Brent retrace sharply to $85–90/bbl, removing the primary near-term bull catalyst.

Bear Case in One Line: Peace in the Gulf would send Brent back to $85–90/bbl, cutting BP's excess FCF by ~$3–4bn/quarter and likely triggering a de-rating to 7x forward earnings.

9. GLOSSARY

Term Plain English
Strait of Hormuz The narrow waterway connecting the Persian Gulf to the Arabian Sea; roughly 20% of global oil exports pass through it, making it the world's single most important oil chokepoint. Any military blockade or disruption instantly reprices energy markets globally.
Risk Premium The extra return investors demand for accepting uncertainty above a "safe" baseline (usually government bonds). When geopolitical risk spikes — as with the US–Iran conflict today — equities and commodities price in a higher risk premium, which simultaneously depresses stock valuations and inflates commodity prices.
Merger Arbitrage Spread The gap between a target company's current share price and the proposed acquisition price. In the GameStop–eBay situation, eBay trades at ~$109 versus the $125 offer — a ~15% spread. Specialist "arb" traders buy the target and short the acquirer, profiting when the deal closes. A wide spread signals the market doubts the deal will complete.
