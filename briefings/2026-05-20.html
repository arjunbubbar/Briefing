RATES & INFLATION PULSE

The US 10-year Treasury yield reached a 52-week high of 4.657% on 19 May, driven by a hotter-than-expected April CPI print (3.8% YoY). Markets have fully priced out any Federal Reserve rate cut for 2026, with futures implying the next potential easing no earlier than December. The Fed funds target range remains 4.25–4.50%; the Richmond Fed's May 11 National Economic Indicators flag robust Q1 GDP (+2.0%) but persistent above-target PCE inflation (3.5% YoY through March).

In the UK, gilt yields continue to face a dual headwind: sticky domestic inflation (CPI 3.3%, rising further as energy costs pass through) and acute political risk. The 10-year gilt yield stands at 5.07% and the 30-year briefly touched 5.81% — its highest level since 1998 — amid speculation over PM Starmer's political survival. The Bank of England voted 8–1 to hold at 3.75% on 30 April; markets are now watching for fiscal credibility signals ahead of the 17 June MPC decision.

GLOSSARY

Gilt
A UK government bond issued by HM Treasury. The name comes from the original gilded edges on bond certificates. Gilt yields rise when bond prices fall — as happens when investors demand more compensation for holding UK government debt (e.g. due to political or fiscal risk). Today the 30-year gilt briefly touched 5.81%, its highest since 1998.
Net Interest Margin (NIM)
The difference between what a bank earns on its loans and investments and what it pays out on deposits, expressed as a percentage of earning assets. A higher Bank Rate generally supports NIM for deposit-funded banks like NatWest, as loan rates reprice faster than deposit costs.
52-Week High (for yields)
When a bond yield reaches its highest level in a year, it signals that the bond price has fallen to a 12-month low — investors are selling. The US 10-year yield at 4.657% today reflects persistent inflation and expectations that the Federal Reserve will keep rates elevated throughout 2026.
