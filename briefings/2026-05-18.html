6. RATES & INFLATION PULSE

US markets are rapidly re-pricing Fed policy: the 10-year Treasury has surged to 4.55% — its highest in nearly a year — after April CPI came in at 3.8% YoY, double the Fed's 2% target. Markets now price a 45% probability of at least one Fed hike before year-end, up from a near-zero probability just one month ago; the Fed Funds target remains at 4.25–4.50% for now. In the UK, the inflation picture is compounded by a political crisis: the 10-year gilt yield has jumped to 5.20%, an 18-year high, as bond markets reprice the risk of a more profligate Labour leadership successor to Starmer. The Bank of England — with Bank Rate at 4.50% — faces a stagflationary squeeze: energy-driven CPI could exceed 6% in early 2027, but a gilt-yield spiral would leave little room for easing even as growth risks mount. BoE Chief Economist Huw Pill continues to signal further tightening, and markets price nearly three additional hikes this year.

Why now: The 9% single-day drop compresses Meta's forward multiple to the low-20s — in line with the broader S&P 500 — despite structurally superior returns on equity and dominant cash generation. Bear case in one line: If AI-driven ad-revenue uplift plateaus before compute costs are recouped, Meta faces a multi-year free-cash-flow drought at historically stretched absolute spending levels.

9. GLOSSARY

Term Plain English
Bond Vigilante An investor who protests loose government fiscal policy by selling bonds en masse, forcing yields higher and borrowing costs up — effectively penalising a government through the market rather than the ballot box. The term was coined by economist Ed Yardeni in the 1980s and is directly applicable to UK gilts today.
Solvency II The EU-derived (now UK-adapted) regulatory framework that governs how much capital insurers like Aviva must hold against their liabilities. When gilt yields spike, the discount rate used to value liabilities rises, which can improve or worsen solvency ratios depending on the asset-liability mix.
Strait of Hormuz A narrow waterway between Iran and Oman through which roughly 20% of globally traded oil passes. When access is disrupted — as it currently is amid the US-Iran standoff — crude prices spike sharply, transmitting energy-cost inflation worldwide and complicating central bank policy.
