RATES & INFLATION PULSE

UK 10-year gilt yields are holding at 5.07% — near post-2008 highs — as markets attach a growing fiscal risk premium to UK sovereign debt. Despite today's CPI reading of 2.8% (the lowest since early 2024), the OBR's Sizewell C warning and the Thames Water impasse have reinforced concerns about public spending trajectories and the prospect of further gilt issuance. Money markets are pricing approximately 45–50bp of Bank of England cuts through end-2026, implying one further 25bp move is near-fully priced. Meanwhile, US 10-year Treasuries remain elevated at 4.67% — a 16-month high — as the Federal Reserve, on hold at 4.25%, signals patience given core PCE data still above target. Bull Points:

  1. Hyperscaler CapEx still accelerating: Microsoft, Google, Meta and Amazon have each guided for record 2026 AI infrastructure spend in recent earnings calls. Nvidia's H200 and Blackwell GPU families are sold out through 2026, with CoWoS packaging capacity at TSMC remaining the primary bottleneck, not demand. (Source: CNBC, company earnings calls)
  2. Data centre revenue compound growth: Nvidia's data centre segment has exceeded analyst expectations for eight consecutive quarters. Consensus for Q1 FY2027 revenue is ~$43bn, up ~80% year-on-year, driven by accelerating Blackwell shipments and growing software/subscription revenues from CUDA and NIM microservices.   3. Sovereign AI wave expanding the TAM: Governments across the EU, Middle East and Asia-Pacific are ordering dedicated national AI compute clusters. This sovereign AI segment, still less than 10% of revenue, is growing at over 200% annually and provides structural demand diversification beyond US hyperscalers. (Source: CNBC, Bloomberg)
  4. Software moat deepening: CUDA's installed base — estimated at over 5 million developers — creates extraordinary switching costs. Nvidia's DRIVE platform for autonomous vehicles and Omniverse for industrial simulation represent multi-billion-dollar optionality not yet reflected in consensus numbers. (Source: Goldman Sachs GSAM Q2 2026 Market Know-How)

Why Now: Earnings catalyst this week with Nasdaq futures +0.62% pre-market suggesting continued AI-sector momentum. The stock has absorbed three straight sessions of macro-driven selling and a re-rating opportunity exists if Blackwell revenue guidance beats the elevated bar.

Key Risks: (1) US export controls on advanced chips to China tighten further, cutting ~15% of addressable market overnight. (2) Any sign of hyperscaler capex moderation in Q2 guidance calls could trigger a sharp de-rating at 32× forward earnings.

Bear in one line: At $6 trillion market cap, Nvidia already prices in near-flawless execution for a decade — any stumble, export restriction, or competing architecture (AMD MI400, Google TPU v6) reprices the multiple fast.

🇬🇧 UK: BP plc (BP.L) — Energy Major Bull Case

BP stands as the most direct UK-listed beneficiary of the Strait of Hormuz disruption, with Brent at $108.80 — a level not sustained since 2022 — generating substantial free cash flow that the market may still be underpricing.

Metric Value
Price (est.)~570p
Market Cap (est.)~£86bn
Fwd P/E (est.)~7×
Dividend Yield (est.)~5.9%

Bull Points:

  1. Sustained high oil price flow-through: BP's upstream free cash flow breakeven is around $40–45/bbl. At $108.80 Brent, the company generates ~$4.5bn additional FCF per quarter versus its base case, providing enormous capacity for buybacks, debt reduction and dividends simultaneously. (Source: Bloomberg, company filings)
  2. Buyback acceleration undervalued by market: BP has signalled $3.5bn in buybacks for H1 2026. At 7× forward earnings, the stock trades at a deep discount to the S&P 500 energy sector multiple, suggesting UK-domestic investor fatigue rather than fundamental weakness. (Source: Bloomberg consensus)
  3. LNG portfolio benefits from TTF recovery: While TTF fell 6.24% today, prices at €48.58/MWh are still ~35% above year-ago levels. BP's integrated gas and LNG segment — roughly 30% of EBITDA — benefits from contracted above-spot volumes and spot exposure via its trading arm. (Source: Trading Economics, company guidance)
  4. Strategic simplification well received: CEO Murray Auchincloss's pivot to oil & gas from renewable overextension has reduced capital destruction concerns that weighed on the stock through 2023–24. Divestments have strengthened the balance sheet, and BP's gearing is now among the lowest in the European major peer group. (Source: Bloomberg)

Why Now: Geopolitical risk premium in oil is structurally elevated while Hormuz remains partially closed. BP is simultaneously a defensive income play (5.9% yield) and a cyclical energy play — a combination rarely available in the FTSE 100 at this valuation.

Key Risks: (1) Diplomatic resolution of the Iran conflict triggers a rapid unwinding of the oil price premium — every $10/bbl fall in Brent cuts BP's annual FCF by ~$3bn. (2) UK windfall tax regime could be extended or deepened, further pressuring domestic profitability.

Bear in one line: BP is an oil-price call option dressed as a value stock — if Hormuz re-opens and Brent retraces to $75–80, the entire FCF-driven bull case evaporates and the dividend becomes the only floor.

GLOSSARY

TTF (Title Transfer Facility)
The benchmark price for natural gas traded in the Netherlands and widely used as the reference for European gas markets. Quoted in euros per megawatt-hour (€/MWh), it serves as Europe's equivalent of Henry Hub in the US. A falling TTF signals reduced supply anxiety or lower demand; elevated TTF (as seen in recent weeks near €50/MWh) typically reflects geopolitical disruption or cold-weather demand spikes.
DXY (US Dollar Index)
A weighted measure of the US dollar's value against a basket of six major currencies: euro (57.6%), Japanese yen (13.6%), British pound (11.9%), Canadian dollar (9.1%), Swedish krona (4.2%), and Swiss franc (3.6%). A rising DXY indicates dollar strength, which typically pressures commodity prices (priced in USD) and emerging market currencies. The DXY is used as a shorthand for broad dollar direction in global macro analysis.
Forward P/E (Price-to-Earnings)
A valuation ratio calculated by dividing a company's current share price by its estimated earnings per share over the next 12 months. Unlike trailing P/E (which uses past earnings), forward P/E reflects market expectations of future profitability. A high forward P/E (e.g., Nvidia at ~32×) implies the market expects strong growth; a low forward P/E (e.g., BP at ~7×) can indicate value or low-growth expectations. Forward estimates are consensus analyst forecasts and subject to revision.
