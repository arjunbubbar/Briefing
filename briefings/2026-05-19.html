🇺🇸 US: NextEra Energy (NEE) — Bull Case

NextEra's proposed acquisition of Dominion Energy would forge America's largest electricity producer at the precise moment AI-driven data-centre demand is creating a structural, multi-decade uplift in power consumption.

  1. Unprecedented Scale: The combined NextEra–Dominion entity would serve approximately 8 million customers across Florida and the mid-Atlantic seaboard, creating unrivalled regulated asset base scale that compresses the per-unit cost of capital and supports a premium regulatory compact — exactly the durable competitive moat equity analysts at J.P.   2. AI Electricity Supercycle: Meta raised its 2026 capex guidance to $125–$145B, Microsoft is expanding aggressively, and Google continues to add hyperscale data-centre capacity — all of which will run on electricity. The Edison Electric Institute estimates US data-centre power demand will grow by 15–20% per annum through 2030. NextEra's Florida Power & Light and the combined Dominion grid are directly in the path of this demand wave. (CNBC, May 2026)
  3. IRA Tax Credit Runway: The Inflation Reduction Act's production and investment tax credits for wind and solar generation extend to 2033, providing a decade-long floor beneath NextEra's renewable development economics. The company's pipeline of wind, solar and battery storage projects totals over 300 GW of potential capacity. (Vanguard 2026 Outlook; IRA statute)
  4. 28-Year Dividend Growth Streak: NextEra has raised its dividend for 28 consecutive years; the Dominion acquisition, once closed, is expected to be accretive to adjusted EPS in year two and could accelerate dividend growth guidance to 10%+ annually through 2028 — rare visibility in an uncertain macro environment. (NEE investor presentations)

Why now: The merger is announced at the intersection of the AI capex supercycle and a regulatory environment broadly supportive of grid modernisation. Rate-regulated utilities like NEE generate bond-like cash flows with equity-like growth optionality — that combination is rare at a moment when bond yields are elevated and equity earnings growth is diverging sharply by sector.

Risks:

  1. Regulatory Drag: DOJ and FERC review of a utility mega-merger of this scale typically takes 12–18 months and almost always involves remedies (divestitures, price caps). A drawn-out process could suppress deal-related upside for an extended period.
  2. Rate Sensitivity: Utilities carry heavy debt loads; if the Fed delivers its priced-in December hike and the long end of the yield curve continues to steepen, NEE's valuation premium (26x forward) could compress materially relative to safer investment-grade bonds.

1-line bear: Regulatory rejection of the Dominion deal combined with a Fed rate cycle that reprices utilities lower would expose NEE's premium multiple to a 30%+ de-rating with limited near-term catalyst to recover it.

  4. UK Energy Security Premium: The UK government's continued licensing of North Sea exploration rounds underpins BP's domestic gas volumes; North Sea gas provides a meaningful hedge against TTF price spikes, and the UK's energy security agenda provides regulatory and political support for continued upstream investment that many ESG-driven peers have ceded. Why now: With UK gilt yields near 18-year highs compressing UK equity valuations broadly, BP at ~9.5x forward earnings represents a multi-decade valuation floor; the dividend yield of ~5.1% is supported by iron-clad FCF at current Brent prices, and the FTSE 100 is still outperforming Wall Street year-to-date — BP, as one of the index's largest constituents, is a direct beneficiary of the rotation into UK value.

Risks:

  2. Transition Liability: Carbon border adjustment mechanisms and accelerating EV adoption in Europe could bring forward the peak-demand tipping point, increasing long-dated stranded-asset risk on BP's North Sea capital commitments.

1-line bear: A successful Iran deal driving Brent to $85, combined with accelerating global EV adoption, would halve BP's free cash flow and force a dividend rebasing within two years.

Glossary

Gilt
A UK government bond issued by HM Treasury, equivalent to a US Treasury. The name comes from the original gold-edged certificates. When gilt yields rise, the price of existing gilts falls — so a "gilt sell-off" means investors are demanding more return to hold UK government debt, often reflecting inflation fears or fiscal concerns.
DXY (US Dollar Index)
A trade-weighted index measuring the US dollar against a basket of six major currencies — the euro (57.6%), yen (13.6%), sterling (11.9%), Canadian dollar (9.1%), Swedish krona (4.2%) and Swiss franc (3.6%). A higher DXY generally tightens financial conditions globally, as commodities priced in dollars (oil, gold) become more expensive for non-dollar buyers.
TTF Gas (Title Transfer Facility)
The benchmark hub for European natural gas trading, located in the Netherlands, priced in €/MWh. TTF is to European gas what Brent is to oil — the reference price that determines energy costs for homes and factories across the continent. Spikes in TTF directly feed through to UK household energy bills and eurozone inflation.
