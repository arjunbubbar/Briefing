The sell-off creates an entry point at a tighter spread to NAV.

Risks: (1) A sustained Iran-driven supply recovery could push Brent toward $85–90/bbl, compressing free cash flow and threatening the pace of buybacks. (2) UK windfall tax legislation and North Sea licensing restrictions remain a domestic regulatory overhang that could constrain capital deployment.

Bear in one line: Full Middle East de-escalation and OPEC+ supply restoration would erode BP's cash generation advantage swiftly, exposing the stock's residual transition-era stranded-asset risks.

9. GLOSSARY

Bank Rate
The benchmark interest rate set by the Bank of England's Monetary Policy Committee. It directly influences the rates banks charge on mortgages and business loans across the UK economy — when the Bank Rate rises, borrowing becomes more expensive; when it falls, credit loosens.
DXY (US Dollar Index)
A measure of the US dollar's value against a basket of six major currencies (euro 57.6%, yen 13.6%, pound 11.9%, Canadian dollar, Swedish krona, Swiss franc). A falling DXY typically benefits commodities priced in dollars — like oil, gold, and copper — because they become cheaper for non-US buyers.
Free Cash Flow Yield
A company's free cash flow per share divided by its current share price. It shows how much cash the business actually generates relative to what investors are paying for it — a high yield can indicate either undervaluation or a mature, cash-generative business, and is the key metric for assessing dividend sustainability without relying on accounting earnings.
