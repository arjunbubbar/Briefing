RATES & INFLATION PULSE

The Bank of England held Bank Rate at 3.75% at its April MPC meeting, with underlying services inflation still running at 4–5% on a three-month annualised basis — well above the 2% target. UK 10-year gilt yields trade at 4.972%, near their highest level since 2008, reflecting sticky domestic inflation, elevated energy-price risk from the Hormuz disruption (Brent above $100 has pushed near-term CPI expectations higher) and persistent gilt supply concerns. Markets now price at most one further 25bp BoE cut by year-end, contingent on a material fall in services inflation and some easing of oil prices.

In the US, the Fed remains on hold at 4.25–4.50%. The 10-year Treasury yields 4.39%, with bonds selling off modestly this morning, partially reversing Friday’s rally. The two-day FOMC meeting begins tomorrow (5 May); markets assign near-zero probability to a rate change, but Chair Powell’s post-decision press conference on Wednesday will be closely watched for guidance on how sustained energy-driven inflation affects the dot-plot path. GLOSSARY

Strait of Hormuz
The narrow waterway between Iran and Oman through which roughly 20% of global oil and a significant share of LNG transit daily. A disruption or closure here creates an immediate supply shock, pushing oil and gas prices sharply higher and weighing on energy-importing economies such as Japan, the UK and Europe. The Hormuz premium currently embedded in Brent is estimated at $15–20/bbl above long-run supply-demand fundamentals.
DXY (US Dollar Index)
A weighted basket measuring the US dollar against six major currencies: the euro (57.6%), Japanese yen (13.6%), pound sterling (11.9%), Canadian dollar (9.1%), Swedish krona (4.2%) and Swiss franc (3.6%). A rising DXY typically pressures commodity prices denominated in dollars and tightens financial conditions in dollar-indebted emerging markets. The index is published by ICE and is the standard global gauge of dollar strength.
Gilt Yield
The annualised return (yield to maturity) on UK government bonds (gilts). When gilt prices fall, yields rise — typically reflecting investor concerns about inflation, fiscal deficits, or the volume of government debt issuance. The 10-year gilt yield approaching 5% signals that bond investors are demanding significant compensation for holding long-duration UK sovereign debt, raising the government’s borrowing costs and tightening financial conditions across the economy.
