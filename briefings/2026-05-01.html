RATES & INFLATION PULSE

UK 10-year gilt yields have climbed to 5.08%, the highest since 2008, reflecting sticky inflation driven by the Iran war oil shock and the BoE's hawkish hold on 30 April — where one MPC member voted for an immediate 25bp hike and several flagged openness to further tightening. The spread over US 10-year Treasuries (4.39%) now stands at roughly +69bp, an unusual premium that reflects UK-specific energy and fiscal risk. Markets have fully priced out Fed cuts for 2026; the FOMC's rare four-member dissent reinforces the "higher-for-longer" narrative, with Fed Funds futures beginning to assign non-trivial probability to a 2027 hike. Short-term UK rate pricing implies the next BoE move is more likely a hike than a cut, with June 2026 seen live.

US: Meta Platforms (META) — Bull Case After the Capex Selloff

One-liner: Meta fell 9% after guiding full-year AI capex to $125–145bn, but the selloff misprices the durable earnings power of the world's most efficient digital advertising machine as it builds infrastructure no competitor can match.

Metric Value
Price (30 Apr close, post-AH)~$540
Market Cap~$1.37tn
Fwd P/E (FY2026E)~21×
Dividend Yield0.5%

Bull Points:

  1. AI monetisation is accelerating, not slowing. Q1 2026 revenue beat consensus, driven by AI-powered ad targeting boosting click-through rates and average revenue per user across Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp. Revenue per user in North America exceeded $70 for the first time. The capex guides spending that underpins this competitive moat. (CNBC, 30 Apr 2026)
  2. The capex multiple compression is a buying opportunity. At ~21× forward earnings, Meta trades at a discount to the S&P 500's tech cohort despite superior free-cash-flow generation. Every prior Meta capex surge (2017 mobile, 2020 cloud) eventually resolved into margin expansion. Bears are extrapolating peak spending as permanent, which history says is wrong. (Bloomberg Markets, Apr 2026)
  3. Reality Labs losses are decelerating and have hidden option value. While VR/AR investment remains a drag, Meta's AI glasses (Ray-Ban collaboration) are selling ahead of expectations and building a wearables ecosystem ahead of potential spatial computing adoption curves. A single hit product could revalue this segment substantially. (CNBC, Q1 2026 earnings)
  4. Buyback and dividend firepower remains enormous. Despite the capex hike, Meta's operating cash flow run-rate exceeds $60bn annually. Management has indicated continued buyback discipline. The 9% selloff has meaningfully improved the buyback yield, making every dollar of repurchase more accretive. (Meta Q1 2026 earnings release)

Why now: The selloff creates an entry point at the lowest valuation since mid-2025. If AI ad monetisation continues to compound at current rates, the heavy capex cycle gets absorbed by revenue growth within 18–24 months, and the stock re-rates materially higher.

Risks: (1) Regulatory action in the EU or US that restricts ad-targeting data use, eroding the algorithm advantage; (2) capex continues to expand beyond guidance, destroying FCF visibility and triggering multiple compression.

Bear case (1 line): If AI infrastructure spending continues to balloon with no clear monetisation timeline, Meta becomes a capex black hole trading at a deserved discount, not a bargain.

UK: J Sainsbury (SBRY.L) — Bull Case Amid War-Impact Warning

One-liner: Sainsbury's shares fell on its war-impact supply chain warning, but the stock offers a defensive income play with a structurally improving food retail market share story that the headline risk obscures.

Metric Value
Price (1 May 2026 open approx.)~286p
Market Cap~£5.8bn
Fwd P/E (FY2027E)~13×
Dividend Yield~4.8%

Bull Points:

  1. Groceries are inflation-pass-through machines in a high-price environment. Elevated food inflation, exacerbated by the geopolitical energy shock, actually helps supermarket revenues even if it compresses near-term margins. Sainsbury's Nectar loyalty pricing and own-label expansion enable margin protection as input costs stabilise. (Morningstar / Alliance News, 1 May 2026)
  2. Market share gains from the mid-market squeeze. As cost-of-living pressures persist, shoppers trade down from premium retailers like M&S Food and Waitrose, and trade up from discounters when quality matters. Sainsbury's "Taste the Difference" range and Argos cross-selling make it uniquely placed to capture both flows. (Bloomberg UK Retail data, Apr 2026)
  3. Attractive income at ~4.8% yield. The dividend is covered by free cash flow and has been maintained through prior inflationary cycles. At a time when gilt yields are elevated, a 4.8% equity yield from a defensive UK consumer staple represents a modest but real premium to cash with growth optionality. (SBRY IR, FY2026 results)
  4. Argos digital and financial services optionality undervalued. Sainsbury's Bank and Argos digital operations are rarely in the investment thesis but represent embedded option value in a UK omnichannel retail landscape where physical footprints are shrinking and click-and-collect dominates. (Sainsbury's corporate reports, 2025–2026)

Why now: The war-impact headline has widened the discount to intrinsic value. Sainsbury's is not a commodity business — it is a branded retailer with pricing power, a loyalty moat, and a 4.8% yield that rewards patience as geopolitical noise fades.

Risks: (1) A prolonged Middle East conflict causes persistent food commodity inflation that cannot be fully passed on, squeezing margins; (2) discounters (Aldi, Lidl) continue structurally taking share, limiting revenue growth.

Bear case (1 line): If the war supply chain disruption is sustained, Sainsbury's margins deteriorate materially while discounters gain share, and the dividend comes under pressure — removing the key investment rationale.

GLOSSARY

TTF (Title Transfer Facility)
The benchmark European natural gas trading hub, located in the Netherlands. TTF prices are the reference rate for wholesale gas contracts across the EU. When TTF spikes — as during the Iran war disruption — it directly feeds into household energy bills and producer input costs across Europe.
FOMC Dissent
When a Federal Open Market Committee member votes against the majority decision on interest rates. Dissents are rare and significant: the April 2026 four-dissent vote was the most divided FOMC since 1992, signalling deep internal disagreement about the inflation and growth outlook — a hawkish signal for markets.
Free Cash Flow (FCF) Yield
A company's free cash flow per share divided by its share price, expressed as a percentage — analogous to a bond yield for equities. A high FCF yield (e.g. 5%+) suggests the stock is generating substantial cash relative to its market value, which can fund buybacks, dividends, or debt repayment. It is widely used as a valuation sanity check alongside P/E ratios.
